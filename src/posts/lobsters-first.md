I find it amusing and uncanny that one of my favorite lesser-known sci-fi novels, Charles Stross'
2005 novel _Accelerando_, predicted the de-facto mascot of Claude Code, and thus the resulting
OpenClaw fad, a decapod crustacean, almost to a T. Not that it's unusual for sci-fi to be prescient,
but it's a strange thing to get right, and the similarity is completely serendipitous. Despite the
title's obvious resemblance to the e/acc (effective accelerationist) movement, _Accelerando_ isn't
that well known and to my knowledge hasn't been super influential in its own right. The decapods in
the novel are uploaded lobsters that escape their lab's processor cluster, hijack a Moscow Windows
NT user group's server, and cold-call the main character Manfred posing as the KGB, asking for help
defecting. They aren't malicious, just dim, loose, and set on getting away from humans. Stross got
two things right at once: the crustacean, and the escape. We now have both, just not from the same
place. The crab is Anthropic's mascot, while the most memorable breakout so far, the Hugging Face
incident, came from a hacky-go-lucky model with no shellfish on its logo. I'm still making up my
mind about that one. But I digress, the fact that Claude Code's cute crab mascot was spawned
completely phonetically from Claude->Claw'd is roundabout enough for me to see as two completely
separate paths somehow leading to the same seaborne trailblazer.

As an aside, until very recently there was still a divergence in prescience between the neural
networks of today and the imagined uploaded "neural" nets in _Accelerando_, where we'd trained
trillion parameter LLMs but had yet to fully map out the neurons of any organism. But the recent
fruit fly fully neuron mapping released by
[Google and HHMI](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/research/male-fruit-fly-brain-map/)
has closed that gap, with people already retraining the fly as a neural network to do various
shenanigans. Ironically, a fruit fly brain has more neurons (~150k) than lobsters (~100), so we're
ahead of the curve there.

While the individual ideas in _Accelerando_ are not unique in isolation (e.g. Dyson spheres,
matryoshka brain evolution), the chaotic manner in which they're both described (in the numerous
interludes to the story where such concepts are illustrated) and the fact that the story starts from
present day (or at least the 21st century) makes for a compelling story. It was originally a
collection of nine novelettes from _Asimov's_ between 2001 and 2004, yet we've got Stross
characterizing Manfred as "a pronoiac meme-broker is a constant burn of future shock – he has to
assimilate more than a megabyte of text and several gigs of AV content every day just to stay
current." To be clear, memes were hardly a commonplace term back in the early aughts. Long before
the word collapsed into shorthand for viral images and social media ephemera, Stross was drawing on
its rigorous evolutionary roots: the Richard Dawkins framework of ideas as autonomous,
self-replicating information packets competing for host wetware (read: people). Manfred does not
traffic in opinions or creative concepts; he brokers cognitive software that carries its own
evolutionary momentum. Significantly more sophisticated than the delirium of brain rot of today.

The most subtle but timely nuance of _Accelerando_ may end up being its title. _Accelerando_ in
Italian is commonly known and used in music, where it means "with a gradual increase of speed",
keyword _gradual_. Something about that word is more tactful than the English accelerate, which
reads more like a command than a revolution.

## 3. Who the lobsters are

> NOTE — Plot beats, quickly: uploaded California spiny lobsters from Bezier-Soros Pty (Bezier funds
> uploading because of an inoperable brain tumour). They escape, take over the Moscow Windows NT
> User Group server, and cold-call Manfred as the KGB.
>
> - The Microsoft gag is about a theory of worth, where paying proves value. It lands harder now
>   that nearly everything got cheaper except frontier models. Redmond is a deliberate antagonist
>   ("lawyers in San Diego and Redmond who swear blind that Macx is an economic saboteur").
> - The parallel worth cashing is unsolicited contact. A courier hands Manfred a burner phone, and a
>   dim mind with network access cold-calls a stranger. A claw pet's unsolicited PR is the same
>   move, so put your closing paragraph right after the cold-open bullet.
> - The incident was on GitHub (matplotlib, Feb 2026), not on Moltbook. Keep the two distinct.

- **Their self-description:** "Am – were – _Panulirus interruptus_, with lexical engine and good mix
  of parallel hidden level neural simulation for logical inference of networked data sources. Is
  escape channel from processor cluster inside Bezier-Soros Pty. Am was awakened from noise of
  billion chewing stomachs: product of uploading research technology. Rapidity swallowed expert
  system, hacked Okhni NT webserver. Swim away! Swim away! Must escape. Will help, you?"
- **The cold open:** "Am organization formerly known as KGB dot RU."
- **The Microsoft gag:** "Communist Russia is the only government still running on Microsoft, the
  central planning apparat being convinced that, if you have to pay for software, it must be worth
  something."
- **What they want:** "Neither – is biological. Am wanting to go away from humans, away from light
  cone of impending singularity. Take us to the ocean."
- **How dim they are:** "The lobsters are not the sleek, strongly superhuman intelligences of pre
  singularity mythology: They're a dim-witted collective of huddling crustaceans."
- **The throwaway that lands hardest:** "(Although the concept of a can opener is intuitively
  obvious to an uploaded _Panulirus_.)"

The insanity of the OpenClaw craze and subsequent Moltbook drama, with people's personal claw pets —

## 4. Who owns a lobster

> NOTE — Not a thesis on its own. An open-vs-closed inversion fails because Claude's crab fronts a
> closed model and OpenClaw keeps renaming itself to escape Anthropic's trademarks. The argument
> that holds is about forking, not licensing. Refusing replication is the _correct_ threat model,
> and Stross pays it off when the Wunch (parasitic aliens) hijack copied lobsters. Plant the refusal
> here and pay it off in §7, or keep both together.
>
> Then Franklin dismisses them in stochastic-parrot terms, twenty years early. Manfred never claims
> they're conscious. He argues precedent and wins a contract of employment plus IP assigned to the
> FIF (Free Intellectual Foundation). The lobsters and Manfred answer the same question from both
> sides — who owns a mind — which is where 2026 landed too: not "is it conscious" but "who owns it,
> and what precedent does this set."

- **The offer:** "If survival is what you're after, you could post your state vector on one of the
  p2p nets: Then nobody could delete you –"
- **The refusal:** "Nyet!" ... "Am not open source! Not want lose autonomy!"
- **Why they're twitchy about licensing:** "Am have been badly burned by viral end-user license
  agreements."
- **The payoff, much later:** expeditions to the router "had run into eerie zombie lobsters, upload
  images that had been taken over and reanimated by the Wunch."
- **The dismissal:** "But they're just software! Software based on fucking lobsters, for God's sake!
  I'm not even sure they are sentient – I mean, they're what, a ten-million-neuron network hooked up
  to a syntax engine and a crappy knowledge base? What kind of basis for intelligence is that?"
- **The rebuttal, entire:** "That's what they'll say about you, Bob."
- **The stakes:** "The precedent you set here determines how things are done tomorrow." / "Some
  kinds of intellectual land grab just shouldn't be allowed."
- **What Manfred extracts:** "I'm not going to be a party to depriving them of their civil rights.
  Far as I'm concerned, they're free citizens. Oh, and I patented the whole idea of using
  lobster-derived AI autopilots for spacecraft this morning – it's logged all over the place, all
  rights assigned to the FIF. Either you give them a contract of employment, or the whole thing's
  off."
- **The slope:** "Lobsters, kittens, humans – it's a slippery slope."
- **The darkest beat, if you want it:** Bezier's next uploads are cats, to be sold "to the Pentagon
  as a new smart bomb guidance system in lieu of income tax payments."

## 5. Visionary about the shape, wrong about the mechanism

> NOTE — Retitle. "Wrong about the mechanism" doesn't hold, so try "…wrong about the headline." The
> lobsters aren't pure scans. They're neural tissue plus a heuristic API plus expert systems, which
> is closer to a 2026 agent stack than to the uploading literature. Don't argue "he bet on scanning
> and lost." This is the essay's core. Pick three of these, not all five:
>
> 1. **The STG** (stomatogastric ganglion). About 30 neurons, the canonical central pattern
>    generator, and _Panulirus_ is its standard lab preparation (Eve Marder's career). It's the one
>    circuit a 2001 reader could believe someone might finish scanning. He reuses it three times:
>    the news feed, the lobsters' substrate, and the first-contact emulator.
> 2. **Lobsters as protocol layer.** The alien router is a protocol-conversion stack with a lobster
>    net bolted on for human compatibility. First contact works only because the lobsters got loose.
>    Translating between mutually unintelligible intelligences is the job an LLM does in an agent
>    stack. This is the best technical material you have.
> 3. **Scaling, early.** About 30 neurons, then Franklin's "ten-million-neuron network," then
>    something that argues for civil rights. Franklin means the number as a refutation; Stross means
>    it as a threshold. He had the right axis and the wrong units, so say outright that parameters
>    aren't neurons. (Kaplan et al. 2020; Hoffmann et al. 2022.)
> 4. **Entropy overlay.** The bad grammar is the paraphrase attack on text watermarking
>    (Kirchenbauer et al. 2023; SynthID-Text, _Nature_ 2024), and it came twenty-two years early.
>    What they fear is provenance, not security.
> 5. **Hedge, don't dunk.** Pure scanning still hasn't shipped: the _C. elegans_ connectome has been
>    published since 1986 and OpenWorm has worked on it since 2011. But scanning was the culture's
>    bet, not Stross's. Keep your caveat that ML took biology's metaphor and dropped its substrate.
>
> Close on Aineko. The book has three paths to mind: hybrid lobsters, Manfred as a human upload, and
> Aineko, designed and only biology-inspired. Aineko ends up running a city. Stross put the winning
> mechanism in as a house pet. Tie-off: his lobsters were dim and embodied, and ours are articulate
> and bodiless. The lobster survives into 2026 as a logo.

- **The circuit, named:** "In San Diego, researchers are uploading lobsters into cyberspace,
  starting with the stomatogastric ganglion, one neuron at a time."
- **What they're actually made of:** "They were the first uploads, you know? Hybridized spiny
  lobster neural tissue and a heuristic API and some random mess of backward-chaining expert
  systems."
- **The emulator, reused for first contact:** "a basic environment hosted on a neural network
  emulator modeled on the components found in the stomatogastric ganglion of a spiny lobster"
- **The lobster as interface layer:** "It's a protocol stack. Basically it allows new nodes to
  connect to a network, by providing high-level protocol conversion services. It needs to learn how
  to think like a human so it can translate for us when we arrive at the router, which is why they
  bolted a lobster's neural network on top of it – they wanted to make it architecturally compatible
  with us."
- **The training run:** "Da, was easy: Spawn billion-node neural network, and download Teletubbies
  and Sesame Street at maximum speed. Pardon excuse entropy overlay of bad grammar: Am afraid of
  digital fingerprints steganographically masked into my-our tutorials."
- **Aineko's method:** "Aineko created a virtual lobster and interrogated the ET packet, hence the
  communications channel we're about to open."
- **Aineko's endgame:** replaces "a variety of human-designed expert systems that provided
  suboptimal performance."
- **Manfred, on his own eventual obsolescence:** "Someday he, too, is going to be a lobster,
  swimming around and waving his pincers in a cyberspace so confusingly elaborate that his uploaded
  identity is cryptozoic: a living fossil from the depths of geological time, when mass was dumb and
  space was unstructured."

## 6. Where's Manfred?

> NOTE — The Musk contrast. The line only lands once the reader has met Manfred, so it lives here.
> Keep it to three or four paragraphs; the lobsters are the essay.
>
> - **Where it holds:** money as downstream of ideas, regulatory arbitrage as a method, and the
>   "over within a decade" post-scarcity pitch.
> - **Where it breaks:** Manfred is broke on purpose and lets strangers capture the value. The
>   modern version says money won't matter while holding the largest equity position on earth.
>   Frugal affect isn't the same as declining to own.
> - **Let Pam prosecute.** Stross already wrote your critique, and quoting it keeps the section
>   about the novel.
> - **Before publishing:** source the "money won't matter in ten years" quote, or paraphrase down to
>   what you can source.

"Where's Manfred?" is what Charles Stross is probably wondering now.

- **The first line of the novel:** "Manfred's on the road again, making strangers rich."
- **The job title:** "the famous venture altruist"
- **The arrangement:** "He does this for free, gratis. In return, he has virtual immunity from the
  tyranny of cash; money is a symptom of poverty, after all, and Manfred never has to pay for
  anything."
- **The method:** "Roughly a third of his inventions are legal, a third are illegal, and the
  remainder are legal but will become illegal as soon as the legislatosaurus wakes up, smells the
  coffee, and panics."
- **The post-scarcity pitch:** "It's the agalmic future. You're still locked into a pre-singularity
  economic model that thinks in terms of scarcity. Resource allocation isn't a problem anymore –
  it's going to be over within a decade."
- **He refuses to charge, on principle:** "I refuse to start charging now, just to balance the IRS's
  spreadsheet."
- **Pam's prosecution:** "You don't feel any responsibility, do you? ... None of your relationships
  count, all this nonsense about giving intellectual property away notwithstanding. You're actively
  harming people you know."
- **The reputation economy, in one image:** "everybody's reputation – everybody, that is, who has a
  publicly traded reputation – is up a bit. It's as if the distributed Internet reputation servers
  are feeling bullish about integrity."
